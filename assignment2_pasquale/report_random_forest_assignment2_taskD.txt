2. Analysis of Model Dynamics:
   - Impact of Tree Depth (max_depth):
     Restricting tree depth to 10 (Config 1) yields slightly higher Planet Precision (79.31% vs 78.81%)
     due to 1 fewer false positive, but increases false negatives from 1 to 2 (reducing Planet Recall
     from 98.94% to 97.87%). Expanding depth to 20 (Config 2) allows the ensemble to capture subtle
     in-transit ingress/egress profiles, boosting Non-Planet Precision to 98.59% (only 1 false negative).
     Increasing depth beyond 20 to 30 or None (Configs 3 and 4) produces identical test predictions and
     confusion matrices, as individual trees naturally exhaust split criteria before reaching depth 30.

   - Impact of Ensemble Size (n_estimators):
     Increasing the number of estimators from 100 to 300 stabilizes decision boundaries significantly,
     raising the Out-of-Bag (OOB) generalization score from 0.7929 to a benchmark-leading 0.8086.
     However, pushing to 500 estimators offers no additional gain in test accuracy (remaining at 86.24%)
     and results in a slight drop in OOB score (0.8043), indicating diminishing returns and marginal
     overfitting to localized photometric noise.

3. Final Verdict:
   Configuration 2 (n_estimators=300, max_depth=20) emerges as the optimal model:
   - It delivers the highest Out-of-Bag generalization score (0.8086) across all tested configurations.
   - It achieves the highest accuracy at the standard default threshold of 0.5 (84.66%).
   - It maintains superior class balance: a high Macro Precision of 88.70%, exceptional Non-Planet
     purity (98.59% precision), and near-total planet detection capability (98.94% recall, missing only
     1 true exoplanet transit out of 94).
   - It avoids the unnecessary computational overhead and potential noise overfitting associated with
     unconstrained 500-tree models (Configs 3 and 4).
